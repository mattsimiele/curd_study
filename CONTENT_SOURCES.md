# Content provenance and scope — 19 October 2026

Curd contains original educational exercises, not copied certification questions. The 27 introductory lessons retain their original identifiers. The 18 applied lessons add 108 questions, three reading cards per lesson, objectives and lesson-level references. All are organized within the same nine Academy of Cheese subject areas.

## Resources actually consulted

- Murray’s 2017 CCP Study Guide, supplied 256-page PDF: milk, production, ripening, assessment, distribution and business topics. References in the app use PDF page numbers, not chapter page numbers.
- ACS Expanded Body of Knowledge, supplied file named 2021, 22 pages; its pages carry a 2017 copyright. Used for the 11 knowledge-domain framework.
- ACS Cheese and Dairy Product Lexicon & Glossary, 2018, 43 pages: sensory definitions and context.
- ACS CCP Exam Blueprint, 2017, one page: distinguishes 11 knowledge domains from six task areas. Its historical task weights are not represented as current exam guarantees.
- Academy of Cheese, Buying and Distribution 101, 13 pages: transport, distribution and product-condition concepts. Its temperature examples are not treated as universal US law.
- Cheeses to Know, supplied three-page list: helped select examples. It is not represented as an official or exhaustive exam list.
- Supplied August–June study timeline: used to associate the existing app categories with domain study months. It is a suggested schedule, not an ACS requirement.

The books recommended inside those documents, including The Oxford Companion to Cheese, American Farmstead Cheese and Mastering Cheese, were not supplied or read in full. Listing a book in a bibliography is not evidence that its contents were reviewed. No claims of complete certification coverage are made.

Primary references checked for selected corrections: FDA Food Code 2022, FDA Listeria guidance, University of Guelph milk-quality and milk-microorganism chapters, the 2025 Comté and French Mont d’Or specifications, and the Uplands, Meadow Creek, WSU Creamery and Roth producer pages. Direct links are in `dist/advanced.js` and displayed in relevant lessons.

## Treatment of supplied shorthand notes

| Note or group | Editorial decision |
| --- | --- |
| Friable = crumbly; buttery diacetyl; tyrosine from casein breakdown | Included with texture/chemistry context; tyrosine is distinguished from calcium lactate. |
| Ideal cave 50–60°F | Qualified: conditions depend on cheese/process; never used as a retail holding rule. |
| Acidic flavour caused by too much moisture | Corrected: moisture/retained lactose can contribute, alongside culture activity, time, temperature and salt. |
| Psychrotrophs grow best in refrigeration | Corrected to ability to grow in the cold, not necessarily optimum growth there. One historical guide also mislabels the term; that error is not repeated. |
| SCC 750,000/mL; >300,000 diagnoses mastitis | Numeric shorthand withheld. Legal limits, species, sampling, quality targets and a clinical diagnosis are different questions. Lessons teach this distinction. |
| Milk arrival/raw holding 45°F, records six months, wet hand milking prohibited | Not turned into universal quiz rules. These require the relevant PMO edition, operation and jurisdiction. Retail lessons cite the separate FDA model-code scope. |
| Listeria grows at 37.4°F; E. coli killed at 160°F; Salmonella most common in meat/eggs | Exact thresholds and simplistic pathogen rankings omitted. Temperature, time, matrix and procedure matter. Included the supported point that Listeria can grow at refrigeration temperatures. |
| Streptococci indicate poor cooling; Streptococcus not found in milk | Contradictory shorthand not used. Streptococcus can occur in milk; a genus name alone does not diagnose a cooling failure. |
| Coliforms killed by pasteurization; environmental/fecal indicators | Avoided a blanket safety inference. Pasteurization does not prevent later contamination; indicators are not a substitute for hazard assessment. |
| Summer milk always low in fat/protein/minerals | Qualified using species, lactation, feed, breed and management. |
| Cow lactation 305 days; cows longest; heifer definition; La Mancha US; Nubian highest fat; East Friesian highest yield; ewe water lowest; sheep milk freezes best | Not added as universal rankings or numerical rules in this release. Breed/species comparison needs a specified population and source; some basic definitions can be added later with focused sourcing. |
| Chymosin most active enzyme | Rephrased as a principal milk-clotting enzyme in calf rennet and many fermentation-produced coagulants; coagulants vary. |
| Stilton sometimes vegetarian; vintage >15 weeks; O’Banon shelf life 10 weeks | Product-specific claims held pending current producer/specification evidence. Shelf life depends on the named product and conditions. |
| Grayson Wales/Ireland; Pleasant Ridge May–October; Cougar Gold Washington; Roth Alpine-style | Included with producer attribution. Inspiration, production season, place and sales availability are distinguished. |
| Comté breeds and 120 days | Included from specification, with minimum versus actual age distinguished. |
| Vacherin Mont d’Or starts 15 August | Scoped specifically to French Mont d’Or / Vacherin du Haut-Doubs; not automatically transferred to Swiss Vacherin. |
| Comté most-consumed protected cheese; Mariano Gonzalez clothbound attribution; Marie Harel; annatto history | Not quizzed as verified historical/statistical claims in this release. Rankings require date/measure; historical attributions need a precise source. |
| Comté/Taleggio/Manchego/Paški sir EU protection | Protected identity is taught, but the complete four-cheese registry claim was not added without checking each current registration. |
| 4 oz = 1 cup grated | Qualified: volume varies by cheese, grater and packing; use weight for consistent portion planning. |
| Restaurant ripeness is #1 | Reframed: ripeness matters to quality, alongside safety, dietary suitability and service requirements. |
| Munster substitutes for Époisses | Not a universal equivalence. Substitutions should compare texture, intensity, milk, budget and customer needs. |

## Limits and publication

The app is an independent study aid. Applied questions are a bridge to deeper study, not an official ACS/Academy exam bank or a full certification course. Lesson references support the topics and explanations; scenarios, amounts and calculations are original instructional examples. FDA Food Code references describe a model code, with local adoption and product exceptions explicitly noted.

The supplied PDFs, extracted text and private timeline remain outside the public deployment. Only original app content and provenance notes are published. Browser progress remains local under `curd-v1`; there is no account sync.

## Cheese library (v0.4)

The supplied *Cheeses to Know* list determines the selection: 106 entries plus Harbison, which is mentioned alongside Winnimere. Every entry has an original three-card identification lesson and four exercises. Twelve three-cheese comparison lessons add six exercises each. References are linked per lesson; producer/consortium references are used where available, with Cheese.com and specialist retailers/publications for many descriptive profiles. This is an identification aid, not an official ACS question bank or complete CCP course.

The profile table is `scripts/cheese-profiles.psv`. Run `python3 scripts/build-cheeses.py` after editing it. Keep existing rows in order because completion IDs use their positions. Append new entries only with a migration for the comparison-lesson IDs. Existing foundation and CCP lesson IDs are unchanged.

Broad names (including Cheddar, Gouda, Robiola and Bergkäse) have qualified descriptions. Sensory traits vary by producer and maturity. Regional/protected examples are identified explicitly. No supplied PDFs, extracted textbook chapters, or private notes are distributed. Exact historical trivia and food-safety rules from the user's notes are not assumed true or added to this identification bank.
